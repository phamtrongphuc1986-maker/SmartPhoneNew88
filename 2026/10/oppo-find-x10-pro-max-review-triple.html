OPPO Find X10 Pro Max Review: Triple 200MP Power in a Familiar Frame

OPPO has never been shy about pushing camera hardware to extremes, and the Find X10 Pro Max is the boldest statement yet. Three 200MP sensors on one phone sounds like marketing fantasy, but OPPO actually built it â€” a 24mm main, a 70mm periscope, and a 13mm ultrawide, all sharing the same headline resolution. Add MediaTek's newest flagship silicon, a silicon-carbon battery that doubles the industry norm, and a China starting price of CNY 6,799, and you have one of the most aggressive flagships of 2026. After testing the phone, here is where it shines and where it stumbles.

Design & Display

The Find X10 Pro Max follows the design language OPPO has refined over the past few generations: a large, gently curved camera plateau centered on the back, slim symmetrical bezels, and a chassis that balances glass and metal nicely in the hand. It is unmistakably a large device, but the balanced weight distribution keeps it comfortable in the hand.

The 6.78-inch AMOLED panel runs at 1.5K resolution with a 144Hz refresh rate, and it is one of the strongest screens on any phone this year. Scrolling is glassy smooth, colors are rich without being cartoonish, and outdoor visibility holds up well under direct sun. Display modes range from vivid to natural and cinematic. The ultrasonic fingerprint sensor sits under the display and is fast and reliable, even with slightly damp fingers.

Durability is a strong point: the phone carries both IP68 and IP69 ratings, so it shrugs off dust, submersion, and even high-pressure water jets. That is genuinely useful peace of mind for anyone who shoots photos in the rain or at the beach.

Performance

The MediaTek Dimensity 9600 Pro, built on a 2nm process, is the other headline here. Paired with up to 16GB of RAM and UFS 4.1 storage (up to 1TB), the phone chews through everything from 4K video editing to the most demanding mobile games without breaking a sweat. In real-world use, multitasking is instantaneous, and the 144Hz display stays fluid even with heavy apps running in the background.

Thermals are well managed. Long gaming sessions warm the upper frame slightly, but there is no throttling drama here â€” sustained performance stays close to peak, which is more than some rival flagships can claim.

Cameras

This is the reason the Find X10 Pro Max exists. The rear system is a triple array where every lens gets a 200MP sensor: a 24mm main with a bright f/1.5 aperture, a 70mm 3x periscope telephoto, and a 13mm ultrawide. The practical benefit of matched sensors is consistency â€” switching between focal lengths produces images with similar color science, detail rendering, and dynamic range, which is rare even among flagships.

Daylight photos from the main camera are superb: crisp detail, natural skin tones, and highlights that roll off gracefully. The f/1.5 aperture pulls in serious light after dark, and OPPO's night processing keeps noise under control without the waxy over-smoothing older models sometimes showed. The 3x periscope is the standout â€” portraits at 70mm have lovely compression and background separation, and distant subjects stay sharp well beyond 3x thanks to the high-resolution sensor.

Video is equally ambitious: 8K recording, 4K at up to 120fps, Dolby Vision HDR, and a proper LOG profile for color grading. The ultrawide is solid rather than spectacular â€” good in daylight, a step behind the main camera at night â€” but it is a worthy third lens rather than a filler.

Battery & Charging

The 8,000mAh silicon-carbon battery is a game changer for heavy users. Two-day battery life is realistic with moderate use, and even power users will end most days with 30â€“40% left. Silicon-carbon chemistry is what makes this possible without a brick-like chassis.

Charging is quick rather than record-breaking: 80W wired charging fills the huge cell in well under an hour, and 50W wireless is fast enough to be genuinely useful â€” a rarity, since many brands still cap wireless speeds much lower.

Software & Features

The phone ships with Android 17 under ColorOS 17. OPPO's software has matured into a clean, customizable experience with a strong set of AI features: photo editing tools, call summaries, and system-wide writing assistance. The interface is smooth and free of the bloat that used to plague Chinese-market software.

Connectivity covers everything expected â€” Wi-Fi 7, Bluetooth 6.0, NFC, and full 5G bands. OPPO has not published a firm update policy for this model, which is worth keeping in mind if long-term software support matters to you.

A global rollout is scheduled for October 21, 2026, though pricing for other regions has not been confirmed yet. Final Verdict

The OPPO Find X10 Pro Max is the most camera-ambitious phone of 2026 and one of the best endurance flagships, period.
