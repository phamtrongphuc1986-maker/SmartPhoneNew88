iPhone Duo Review: Apple's First Foldable Phone (2026)

Apple is fashionably late to foldables â€” and characteristically confident about it. Announced on September 9, 2026 alongside the iPhone 18 series, the iPhone Duo is Apple's first folding phone. Preorders open October 16, with sales starting October 23, and it arrives at a steep $1,999.

Folding phones have matured over several generations of Android competition, so the Duo doesn't get a pass for first-generation awkwardness. It has to be great on day one. Here's our full review.

Design & Display

The iPhone Duo's headline act is its folding design. Open, it reveals a 7.6-inch inner OLED display â€” essentially a small tablet in your pocket. Closed, a 5.4-inch outer display handles quick tasks, calls, and notifications without unfolding.

Thickness is impressively managed: just 5.2mm when open and 11.3mm when closed. At 254g it's weighty but balanced, and the hinge feels superbly engineered â€” smooth, reassuring, and free of the gritty looseness that plagued early foldables. Apple has clearly studied the category's failures before entering it.

The inner display is the experience that justifies the price. Reading, multitasking with split-screen apps, photo editing, and video watching all feel transformative at this size. The crease is visible under direct light at certain angles, but in normal use it fades into the background â€” a notable achievement.

The 5.4-inch outer display is more than a stopgap. It's bright, sharp, and fully usable for messaging and calls, meaning you only unfold when the task deserves it. This two-screen discipline is key to the Duo's battery story.

Performance

The Duo runs the same Apple A20 Pro chip found in the iPhone 18 Pro series, so performance is flagship-grade. Multitasking across the large inner display â€” three apps on screen, picture-in-picture video, drag-and-drop â€” is handled without a hint of lag.

Connectivity gets new silicon: the C2 cellular modem and N1 wireless chip. In our testing, 5G speeds were excellent and Wi-Fi performance rock-solid. Apple designing its own modem stack for a foldable, where antenna placement is tricky, shows real engineering confidence.

Cameras

Here's the compromise. The Duo carries a dual rear camera system: a 48MP main sensor and a 48MP ultrawide â€” but no telephoto lens. In a $1,999 device, the missing zoom lens is the most conspicuous absence.

What you get is still very good. The 48MP main camera produces the same high-quality images as its iPhone 18 siblings, with strong dynamic range and Apple's reliable color science. The ultrawide is sharp and consistent. But portrait shots and distant subjects suffer without optical zoom â€” you'll lean on digital zoom sooner than you'd like on a phone at this price.

The inner display also changes how you shoot: framing photos on a 7.6-inch canvas is genuinely delightful, and reviewing shots immediately after capture feels like using a dedicated camera's rear screen.

Battery & Charging

Apple rates the Duo at up to 44 hours of video playback on the outer display and 31 hours on the inner display. That split rating tells the real story: the big inner screen is the battery hog, and how you use the phone determines your endurance.

In mixed real-world use â€” mostly outer display with a few hours of inner-screen time â€” the Duo comfortably lasts a full day. Heavy inner-display sessions (long video streaming, gaming) will have you charging by evening. Plan your usage accordingly, and the battery is a non-issue.

Software & Features

One of the Duo's most interesting choices is Touch ID instead of Face ID. Without a notch or Dynamic Island on the folding displays, Apple opted for a side-mounted fingerprint sensor. It's fast and reliable, and honestly, Touch ID on a foldable makes ergonomic sense â€” your thumb naturally rests on the sensor when unfolding.

iOS adapts well to the folding form factor, with continuity between the outer and inner displays: start a task on the small screen, unfold, and it expands seamlessly onto the big one. Multitasking gestures are intuitive, and most major apps already support the large-screen layouts.

Price & Availability
